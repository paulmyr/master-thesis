\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}

\Cref{sec:discussion:klos} already mentioned investigating the use of different neuron models
--- in the context of surrogate-gradient based optimisation and using differentiable models.
This would allow us to understand better the contribution of surrogate biases.

A second very interesting future work topic would be designing and evaluating different loss functions.
Investigating efforts that might have been established in Signal-Processing, different areas of \gls{ML},
or other research areas could lead to interesting transfer applications.
Better understanding the affect of loss functions when gradient-based optimisation works well
(e.g. in the context of using \gls{HH}-type models) might provide valuable insights.
In the context of feature-based losses we mentioned the problem of too many hyperparameters.
This is made worse when taking feature weighing into account --- this is a problem of multi-objective optimisation.
Pareto fronts have not been explored or discussed in the context of this work due to time constraints.
They might help 

Analysing different stabilisation techniques to reduce vanishing/exploding gradients could 
most likely improve the qualitative results of this method.
Using multi-trace optimisation could help with the under-constrained fundamental problem of fitting the AdEx
model to spike timings, which is an essential next step in the task of neuron model fitting.

\section{Reflections}
\label{sec:reflections}

% Don't forget that there must be a chapter dealing with aspects of your work and ethics,
% sustainability and relations with the surrounding community.

The work does not raise ethical concerns.
All neuron recordings are simulated or sourced from the publicly released Johansson \& Silberberg recordings under their original ethics approval.
No animal experiments or were performed for this thesis.
Any positive results might actually help to reduce need of real recordings by more enabling more accurate,
large-scale brain simulations.

The most economically and socially relevant aspect of this work is the negative result itself.
\gls{HPC}-scale gradient-based parameter fitting of biophysical neuron models,
as exemplified by \texttt{Jaxley}~\cite{Deistler2025},
is an attractive proposition precisely because GPU acceleration and automatic differentiation promise orders-of-magnitude
speedups over derivative-free methods on Hodgkin--Huxley-type models.
This thesis provides evidence that, for \gls{AdEx} at least, that transfer is not automatic.
Researchers planning to commit compute and engineering effort to surrogate-gradient pipelines for biophysical fitting
should factor this in;
However, the provided implementation also enables GPU-based implementations of different optimisation methods
through the underlying \texttt{JAX} layer.

Energy consumption was kept to a minimal.
Almost all computations were performed on a single Apple M1 Macbook.
